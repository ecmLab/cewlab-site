CEW Lab

Clean Energy and Water Laboratory · Department of Mechanical Engineering · Kate Gleason College of Engineering · Rochester Institute of Technology

Materials set what a cell could store. Mechanics decides how long it survives storing it.

The Clean Energy and Water Laboratory studies how solids deform while they react — the coupling that limits solid-state batteries and separation membranes — at RIT's Kate Gleason College of Engineering.

Our research Join the lab

A battery is a structure that changes shape while it works. Lithium plates into a ceramic that cannot accommodate it, cathode particles swell and crack away from their conductive network, and a separator that was mechanically sound at assembly is fractured after fifty cycles.

In ionic ceramics, electrodes, and ion-gated microelectronics, microstructure and properties evolve while the device operates — so the processing–structure–property relation never closes. We ask what stress does to a material reaction when the constitutive response is itself a state variable, and how the competition between mechanics and electrochemistry decides performance.

We call that question reactive mechanics, and we pursue it by modeling and by experiment — then apply it across energy storage, chip-scale microelectronics, and environmental separation and critical-element recovery.

Research thrusts

  • Reactive mechanics

    One constitutive law for a reacting solid: stress feeding back into reaction rate, granular creep during densification, and a thermo-electro-chemo-mechanical framework with an AI surrogate.

    Read more →

  • Energy storage

    Solid-state cells fail mechanically before they fail electrochemically. Dendrite fracture, composite-cathode accommodation, and dry-processed scale-up.

    Read more →

  • Microelectronics

    Electrochemistry on the chip: ECRAM for neuromorphic computing, single-particle measurement at picoamp-hour resolution, and lithographically defined micro-batteries.

    Read more →

  • Separation & recovery

    Electro-reactive deionization for industrial wastewater, membrane-free separation with aqueous ion traps, and closed-loop recovery of rare earths.

    Read more →

We are hiring

The CEW Lab has openings for MS and PhD students and postdoctoral researchers in both research directions. Experience with electrochemistry is welcome but not required to start — we look for a background in solid or fluid mechanics and in continuum or atomic-scale modeling.
